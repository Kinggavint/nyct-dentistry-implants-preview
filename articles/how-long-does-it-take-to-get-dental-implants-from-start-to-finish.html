Dental implants

How long do dental implants take, start to finish?

Getting an implant is a process, not a single appointment. Here is what happens at each stage and why the timeline is different for everyone.

From the Dentistry & Implants team

The short answer

For most patients, going from first visit to final tooth takes several months. Some people finish sooner, and some need more time. The reason is simple: the implant has to bond with your jawbone, and bone heals on its own schedule. We would rather give you an honest range after your exam than a promise we cannot keep.

Step 1: Exam and planning

Your first visit is a consultation. We review your health history, look at your gums and teeth, and take 3D scans so we can see your bone. Dr. Kwon uses what he sees to plan the position of each implant. If you are comparing options, our page on dental implants explains what is available.

Step 2: Getting ready, if needed

Some mouths need preparation first. A damaged tooth may need to be removed, gum disease may need to be treated, or the bone may need to be built up with a bone graft. These steps add healing time before the implant, which is the biggest reason one timeline is longer than another.

Step 3: Placing the implant

The implant itself is placed in a short visit, with the area numbed. In some cases a temporary tooth can be attached the same day. Not everyone qualifies for that, and we explain who does in our guide to same-day implants.

Step 4: Healing

This is the longest part. Over the following months, the bone grows around the implant and holds it firmly. During this time you are not simply waiting. You have follow-up visits so we can check how you are healing, and you will have a temporary solution if the implant is in a spot you can see.

Step 5: The final crown

Once the implant is secure, we take impressions or scans and make your custom crown. A visit or two later, the crown is attached and adjusted until your bite feels right. Then you have a tooth that looks and works like the others.

Staying comfortable and informed along the way

Waiting months can feel long, so we try to make the in-between easy. You will know what the next visit is for before you leave the current one, and you can call us if something feels off. If you have a gap in your smile during healing, ask about a temporary tooth so you are not hiding your smile.

It also helps to look after your mouth while you wait. Keep brushing and cleaning between your teeth, avoid chewing hard foods on the healing site, and skip smoking if you can. Those small habits support the bone as it heals and can keep your plan on schedule.

What changes your timeline

  • How many teeth you are replacing.
  • Whether a tooth needs to come out first.
  • Whether you need a bone graft or gum treatment.
  • Your general health and how you heal.
  • Whether your case is a good fit for a same-day approach.

If you are replacing most or all of your teeth, our full-arch implants page explains how that process works. Costs and timing are tied together, so it helps to look at the insurance and financing page early.
