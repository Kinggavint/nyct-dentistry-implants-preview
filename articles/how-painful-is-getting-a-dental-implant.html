Dental implants

How painful is getting a dental implant?

It is one of the most common questions we hear, and the answer is more reassuring than most people expect.

From the Dentistry & Implants team

During the procedure

Your mouth is numbed with local anesthetic before the implant is placed, so you should not feel pain while the work is being done. You may feel pressure or vibration, which is normal. If you tell us anything feels sharp, we stop and numb you further.

If you are nervous, tell us when you book. At our Kent office, nitrous oxide (laughing gas) can help you feel more relaxed, and we will talk through what is right for you.

After the numbness wears off

Most patients describe the first days as sore rather than painful. Common experiences include:

  • Tenderness around the implant site.
  • Mild swelling, and sometimes bruising on the cheek or gums.
  • A little bleeding or oozing in the first day.

This usually eases over several days. Many people manage it with over-the-counter pain relievers as directed by their dentist, and some patients are given a prescription. Your experience can differ, especially if bone grafting or several implants were part of the visit.

What helps you heal comfortably

  • Use cold packs on your cheek the first day, in short intervals.
  • Eat soft foods and avoid chewing on the implant site.
  • Rinse as instructed, and avoid anything that disturbs the area, such as spitting hard or using a straw early on.
  • Rest, and skip strenuous exercise for a few days.
  • Do not smoke, which slows healing.

Is an implant worse than an extraction?

Many patients are surprised to find that an implant is more comfortable than they expected, and often comparable to having a tooth removed. A single implant placed in a healthy site is usually a short procedure. More involved cases, such as grafting or full-arch treatment, can mean more soreness afterward.

When to call us

Some soreness is normal. Call us if pain gets worse after the first few days instead of better, if swelling keeps growing, if you have a fever, or if bleeding does not stop with gentle pressure. We will want to see you.

Fear is normal, and it is fixable

If the idea of implant surgery worries you, that is very common. Telling us early lets us plan numbing and the pace of the visit around you. Many patients tell us they wish they had asked sooner, because the real procedure was calmer than the one they pictured.

Questions patients often ask

Will I be awake? Yes, with the area numbed. If you are anxious, our Kent office offers nitrous oxide (laughing gas), which helps you relax while you stay awake.

How long until I feel normal? It varies. Many people return to routine activity within a few days, while the gums and bone keep healing for longer underneath. We will tell you what to expect for your own case.

Can I go to work afterward? Many patients plan a day or two of rest, especially after a larger procedure. Ask us when you book so you can plan your week.
