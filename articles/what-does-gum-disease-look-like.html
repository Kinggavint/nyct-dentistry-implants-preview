Periodontal care

What does gum disease look like

Gum disease often starts quietly. Knowing the early signs gives you a strong chance of stopping it before it harms your teeth.

From the Dentistry & Implants team

Why it is easy to miss

Early gum disease is often painless. Many people only notice a little blood in the sink, or assume sore gums are normal. Dentists describe gum disease in stages, and each has its own signs.

Stage one: Gingivitis

Gingivitis is the earliest stage, and it can be reversed with good care. You may notice:

  • Gums that are swollen or puffy
  • Gums that look dark red or purple instead of pink
  • Bleeding when you brush or floss
  • Persistent bad breath

At this point the bone and tissue that hold your teeth are still intact. A professional cleaning and better daily habits can often bring your gums back to health.

Stage two: Periodontitis

If gingivitis is left alone, it can progress to periodontitis. Now the infection has moved below the gum line, and damage to the supporting tissue and bone is not fully reversible. It can usually be controlled with treatment. Signs include:

  • Gums pulling back, so teeth look longer
  • Tender or painful gums
  • Teeth that feel sensitive
  • Discomfort when chewing

Advanced periodontitis

In the advanced stage, the structures that hold teeth in place have been seriously damaged. You may notice:

  • Loose or shifting teeth
  • A change in how your teeth fit together when you bite
  • A bad taste in your mouth
  • Pus between the teeth and gums
  • Pain when eating

Some teeth can still be saved at this stage, but others may need to be removed and replaced. This is why catching it sooner matters.

What to do if you see these signs

Do not wait for pain. Book a visit so a dentist can measure your gums, take X-rays if needed, and tell you what stage you are in. Treatment often starts with a deep cleaning, which removes bacteria from below the gum line. Some cases need more advanced care. Our periodontal care page explains what is available.

Why early care matters

The sooner gum disease is treated, the more likely it is that you can keep it under control and keep your own teeth. Gum disease has also been linked with other health concerns, so it is worth taking seriously even when it does not hurt. If you are not sure whether what you see is normal, a quick exam can settle the question and give you peace of mind.

Habits that protect your gums

You can lower your risk with simple routines:

  • Brush twice a day for two minutes and clean between your teeth daily
  • Keep your regular checkups and cleanings
  • Limit sugary drinks
  • Avoid smoking, which makes gum disease more likely and harder to treat

Regular preventive care is the easiest way to catch problems early.
