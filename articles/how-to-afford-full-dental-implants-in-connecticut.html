Full-Arch Implants

How to Afford Full Dental Implants in Connecticut

Replacing most or all of your teeth is a big decision, and cost is part of it. Here are practical ways to plan.

From the Dentistry & Implants team

Why Full Implants Take Planning

Full-arch implants replace a whole row of teeth using a few strategically placed implants. They are a significant treatment, and the total depends on your case. We explain more on our full-arch implants page. What matters here is that the cost is not one fixed number, and there are ways to approach it.

What Shapes the Cost

  • Whether you need one arch or both.
  • The condition of your bone, and whether grafting is needed.
  • Whether teeth must be removed first.
  • The type of replacement teeth chosen.
  • What your insurance covers.

After an exam and scans, we give you a written estimate that separates what insurance is expected to pay from what you would pay.

Start With Your Insurance

Dental plans often have yearly limits, and implants may be covered only in part. Some plans help with extractions, bone grafts or the restoration. We check your benefits before your visit so you know where you stand. Coverage varies by employer and plan, so the office will verify yours.

Spread the Cost Over Time

Payment plans through Cherry and CareCredit are available for qualified applicants, and some offer 0% financing. Our Stamford office also accepts Sunbit, Chewsi and GreenSky. You can usually use HSA and FSA funds for dental treatment too. Everything is listed on our insurance and financing page.

If You Do Not Have Insurance

Our Stamford office has an in-house membership plan with no deductibles, waiting periods, claims or yearly maximums. It covers routine care and includes savings on other treatment. Ask the office how it applies to your plan.

Look at New Patient Specials

Our Stamford office currently offers $1,000 off dental implants for new patients who need to replace missing teeth, once an exam confirms implants are right for you. Offers cannot always be combined, so call to confirm eligibility.

A Realistic Way to Plan

Many patients combine several of these tools: insurance for the parts it covers, a payment plan for the rest, and a special if they qualify. Taking a few days to see the written estimate and compare it with your budget is perfectly fine. We would rather you feel sure than rushed.

Questions to Bring to Your Consultation

It helps to arrive with a few questions. Ask what the written estimate includes, which parts your insurance may cover, which payment plan options fit your budget, and whether a staged plan makes sense. You can also ask how long each stage takes, since timing and cost are linked.

Our team is used to these conversations, and we would rather you ask than guess. The goal is a plan you understand and feel comfortable with, whichever route you choose.

Think About the Whole Picture

Dentures, bridges and implants have different upfront and long-term considerations. Dr. Kwon will talk through your options honestly, including whether a different approach, such as dentures, would suit you better right now. There is no pressure to decide at your first visit.
