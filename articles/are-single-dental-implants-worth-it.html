Dental implants

Are single dental implants worth it?

A single implant is a real investment. Here is how it compares with the other ways to replace one tooth, so you can decide if it is worth it for you.

From the Dentistry & Implants team

What a single implant replaces

When you lose a tooth, you lose two things: the part you see and the root underneath. A bridge or a denture replaces only the visible part. A single implant replaces the root too. A small titanium post is placed in the jawbone, and once it has bonded with the bone, a custom crown is attached on top.

That is the main reason many people find implants worth considering. The jawbone is kept busy by a root, and without one the bone in that spot can slowly shrink. An implant gives the bone something to hold on to.

What you get for the money

Here is what patients and dentists tend to value most in a single implant:

  • It stands on its own. A traditional bridge needs the teeth on either side to be filed down to hold the new tooth. An implant leaves those healthy neighbors alone.
  • It looks and feels natural. The crown is made to match the color, shape and size of your other teeth.
  • Daily care is simple. You brush and floss it the way you do your own teeth, and keep up with regular checkups.
  • It is made to last. With good care, an implant often lasts many years, and the crown on top may eventually need replacing.
  • Eating and speaking feel normal. Nothing slips or needs to come out at night.

Compared with dentures, which can shift and need constant upkeep, a single implant is a steadier everyday experience. If you are comparing options, our page on single-tooth implants walks through how the treatment works.

What it costs

The price of a single implant depends on your case: the condition of your bone, whether a tooth needs to be removed first, the materials used, and the work involved in planning and placing it. We will not quote a number in an article, because it would not be honest to guess before an exam.

What we can tell you is how the money side works. Many dental plans limit or exclude implant coverage, so we check your benefits and give you a written estimate before treatment. Cherry payment plans with 0% financing for 12 to 18 months are available to qualified applicants. See insurance, financing and specials for the details.

When an implant may not be the right choice

Implants are not for everyone, and they are not always the first answer. If gum disease is active, or the bone is thin, you may need treatment first. Some health conditions and habits, such as smoking, can slow healing. In some cases a bridge or a partial denture is the wiser path, and a careful dentist will tell you so.

Dr. GiTae Kwon is an implant surgeon who also teaches implant surgery to other dentists. He saves natural teeth whenever he can and recommends an implant only when it is the better choice for you.

So, is it worth it?

For many people who are missing a single tooth, an implant is worth it because of what it protects: your neighboring teeth, your bone and your daily comfort. For others, a different option fits better. The honest answer comes from an exam, scans and a conversation about your goals and budget.

The full process usually takes several months from start to finish, because the implant needs time to bond with the bone. Your consultation will cover the timeline for your case.
