Preventive care

Prevention Is the Foundation of a Healthy Smile

The easiest treatment is the one you never need. Regular exams and cleanings catch small problems early, keep gums healthy and help you avoid bigger, costlier work later.

Book a ConsultationCall an office

Why it matters

What preventive care does

Tooth decay and gum disease are the most common causes of tooth loss, and both are largely preventable. A visit every six months lets us remove plaque and tartar you cannot reach at home, check every tooth and your gums, and spot trouble while it is still small.

An exam can also reveal things beyond your teeth. Dentists look for early signs of oral cancer, and changes in the mouth sometimes point to wider health concerns such as diabetes. Gum health and overall health are connected, so a healthy mouth supports a healthy body.

Staying ahead of problems usually means less time in the chair and fewer surprises. That is the whole idea.

What we offer

Everything in a checkup

Preventive care is simple, thorough and gentle.

  • Cleanings and exams

    A professional cleaning about every six months removes plaque, tartar and surface stains. A thorough exam looks for decay, gum disease and bite concerns.

  • X-rays and diagnostics

    Digital X-rays and intraoral cameras let you see what we see. Our Mount Kisco and Kent offices also use laser cavity detection.

  • Fluoride treatment

    A fluoride gel or paste is applied to the teeth for a few minutes. It attracts minerals and strengthens enamel, even where the earliest stage of decay has begun.

  • Dental sealants

    A thin protective resin is painted onto the grooves of back teeth and hardened with a light. It blocks bacteria, acid and food from settling in and causing cavities.

  • Oral cancer screening

    A screening is part of your regular exam. We look for lesions, discolored patches and other changes that deserve a closer look, which matters most if you smoke or spend long hours in the sun.

  • Advice that fits you

    Dr. Kwon and Dr. Park talk through your habits and answer your questions, so you leave knowing what to keep doing and what to change.

Your part

Small habits, big difference

Your daily routine matters as much as your visits. Brush at least twice a day for two minutes with fluoride toothpaste, and floss once a day to remove debris brushing misses. An antiseptic mouthwash can help.

Food and lifestyle count too. Cutting back on sugary drinks and constant snacking lowers your risk of cavities and gum disease. Reducing alcohol and avoiding smoking, vaping and oral tobacco protect your gums and your overall health.

If a cleaning shows early gum trouble, we explain it plainly and may suggest periodontal care so it does not progress.

Common questions

Good things to ask

How often should I visit?

Most people do well with a cleaning and exam about every six months. If you have gum disease or a higher risk of cavities, we may suggest visiting more often.

What happens during a cleaning?

We remove plaque and tartar, polish your teeth and check your gums. The dentist then examines your teeth, gums and mouth and reviews any X-rays with you.

Are dental X-rays necessary?

X-rays show decay between teeth, bone levels and problems under the gums that cannot be seen by eye. We take only the ones your exam calls for.

Do cleanings hurt?

Most people feel little or nothing. If your gums are tender, tell us and we will adjust, and we can use numbing if you need it.

What are dental sealants?

Sealants are thin protective coatings placed on the chewing surfaces of back teeth to help prevent cavities. They are quick and painless.

Can prevention save money?

Often, yes. Treating a small problem early is usually simpler than repairing a large one. Ask the office about your own costs, or see our insurance and financing page.
