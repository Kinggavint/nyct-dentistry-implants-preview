Tooth Extractions

Gentle Extractions, Explained First

We treat extraction as a last resort and explore every option first. When a tooth cannot be saved, we remove it gently and talk with you about replacing it.

When It Is Needed

Why a Tooth May Need to Come Out

Some teeth are too damaged by decay, infection, gum disease or injury to be saved with a root canal or a crown. Others, like many wisdom teeth, simply have no room. In those cases, removing the tooth ends the pain and protects the teeth around it.

We always explore other treatment first. Curious how we decide? Read how bad a tooth needs to be to be extracted.

Types

Four Kinds of Extraction

The right type depends on the tooth and where it sits.

  • Simple extractions

    For teeth you can see above the gum line. The tooth is numbed, loosened and gently removed.

  • Surgical extractions

    For broken or impacted teeth. A small opening in the gum lets the dentist remove the tooth, sometimes in pieces.

  • Wisdom teeth

    Wisdom teeth usually arrive late, between about 17 and 25, and many mouths lack space. Removal can prevent pain, crowding and infection.

  • Urgent extractions

    A severely damaged or infected tooth may need to come out right away. Call and we will arrange a visit.

Wisdom Teeth

A Surgeon Who Teaches It

Wisdom tooth removal can be straightforward or complex, especially when a tooth is partly covered by gum or bone. Dr. Kwon holds a Master's degree in Dental Implantology and Advanced Surgical Extractions. He is part-time faculty at the Gangnam International Implant Academy (GIIA), where he teaches wisdom tooth extraction to fellow dentists.

Your mouth is numbed and sedation can be added at our Stamford office if you need it, so ask the office about your options. For an impacted tooth, a small incision is made, the tooth is removed and the area is cleaned and closed with stitches.

Healing

What to Expect Afterward

  1. The first days

    Rest, and follow the instructions you receive. Some soreness, bleeding and swelling is normal at first.

  2. About a week

    Most of the healing happens in the first week for many patients, and symptoms settle.

  3. Two weeks or more

    Your mouth usually feels mostly normal after about two weeks, though healing varies with your health and how many teeth were removed.

  4. Protect the site

    Following your instructions closely helps you avoid complications such as dry socket. Call us if pain gets worse instead of better.

Replacing a Tooth

Afterward, Replace It

A gap can let neighboring teeth drift and make chewing harder. We talk through replacement before you leave, including dental implants, bridges and dentures.

Common Questions

Good Things to Ask

Does an extraction hurt?

The area is numbed, so you should feel pressure but not sharp pain. Ask the office about sedation if you are anxious.

How long is recovery?

Most people feel mostly normal in about one to two weeks. Your dentist will give you written instructions.

Can I eat normally afterward?

Start with soft foods and avoid the extraction site at first. Your instructions will say when to return to your normal diet.

Do all wisdom teeth have to come out?

No. Teeth that are healthy, fully erupted and easy to clean can often stay. An exam and X-ray tell us what is right for you.

What if I have a problem afterward?

Call the office. Worsening pain, swelling or bleeding should be checked, and we will tell you what to do.
